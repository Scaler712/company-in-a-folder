# Visual style

Written by you. The AI never invents a brand rule.

Set by Vera and Omar on 2027-01-15, after the second time a supplier printed the wrong blue.

## Colours

| Colour | Code | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Deep harbour blue | #10344F | The main colour. Logo, headings, the flat background on every ad |
| Signal orange | #E8702A | One accent only, on the button or the one word that matters. Never a background |
| Warm off white | #F4F1EA | Page and slide backgrounds. Never pure white, it glares on a phone |
| Slate | #46525C | Body text, captions, table lines |
| Ink | #14181B | Only for small print and anything printed at under 10 millimetres |

Two colours per piece, plus the off white. Three is already too many.

## Fonts

- Headings: Fairhaven Grotesk Bold. Licensed for the shop and for print, licence PDF on the
  shared drive under brand, folder 2027 brand.
- Body text: Tidewater Text Regular, and Medium for table headers. Same licence folder.
- Nothing else, ever. If a font is missing, the piece waits. Omar does not substitute.

## Logo

- Files live on the shared drive under brand, folder logo, in SVG and PNG at three sizes, plus a
  one colour version for vinyl cutting.
- Smallest allowed: 24 millimetres wide in print, 90 pixels wide on screen. Below that use the
  one colour mark on its own without the words.
- Clear space around it: the height of the letter T in the logo, on all four sides.
- Never: stretch it, rotate it, add a shadow or an outline, recolour it outside the five colours
  above, put it on a photo without the off white panel behind it, or retype the words in another
  font.

## References

Four pieces we want new work to sit beside. Described, not linked, because the point is the
quality and not the company.

1. A hardware shop's price card: one price per line, huge numerals, nothing else on the card.
   What we like: it trusts the reader to read a number and leaves the rest of the card empty.
2. A ferry timetable poster from the 1960s: two colours, heavy headline, generous margins. What
   we like: the density. It carries a lot of information and still looks calm.
3. A bakery's van livery seen on the main road: the name across the full length of the panel,
   one line, no strapline, no list of services. What we like: it is readable at 40 miles an hour,
   which is the only test a van has to pass.
4. A machine tool catalogue spread: a part photographed straight on, on a plain background, with
   a short caption under it. What we like: the photography does the selling and the words only
   say what it is.

## Never

- Stock photos of people shaking hands or pointing at a laptop. They are not our crew and nobody
  believes them.
- Photographs of a finished job taken at an angle in the rain. Straight on, dry, daylight, or
  it does not go out.
- Gradients, glows, bevels or drop shadows on type. We cut vinyl for a living, so flat is honest.
- Before and after sliders. Two photos side by side at the same size instead.
- The words "solutions", "bespoke", "reach out" and "we would love to". The same words are
  already banned in writing (`core/business.md`).
- Any claim that is not in `marketing/proof.md`. A visual is copy too.
