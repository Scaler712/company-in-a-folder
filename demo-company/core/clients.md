# Clients

This file decides what every bot chases and what it turns down.

## Who we sell to

- Type of business: local businesses with a shop front, a van fleet, or events to promote.
  Dentists, cafes, gyms, plumbers, builders, vets, hardware stores, clubs.
- Size: 2 to 50 staff. Enough to have a budget, small enough that the owner decides.
- Where: Fairhaven and the towns along the bay, roughly a 40 mile drive from the shop. Install
  jobs further out need the owner to agree first, because a crew day is a crew day.
- Who decides: the owner, or the office manager with the owner's budget.
- The problem they come to us with, in their words: "our sign looks tired and the new place next
  door looks better than us", "we bought three vans and they are still white", "we need a banner
  for Saturday and the last one faded in a month".

## Our best 3 clients and why (no names needed)

1. A dental practice that redid its windows, then came back for an A-frame and a waiting room
   sign. Good fit: one office manager decides, they sign proofs the same day, they pay on time.
2. A plumbing firm with 9 vans. Good fit: repeat work as vans are replaced, and they book the
   wrap bay weeks ahead instead of ringing on a Friday.
3. A rowing club that orders two event banners every season. Small money, but they order on a
   calendar we can see, and they recommend us to every club on the water.

## Signs of a good fit

- They have more than one vehicle, or more than two windows, so the job is worth a crew trip.
- They talk about a date that is more than two weeks out.
- They already have a logo file and know where it lives.
- They ask what it will look like, not only what it costs.

## Who we turn down

One line each: who, and the reason. A bot quotes these lines word for word when it says no.

- one-off personal jobs under our $150 minimum order, because the setup and artwork time costs
  more than the job earns
- rush jobs we would have to deliver in under 48 hours, because every rush job pushes two booked
  jobs late
- political campaign work, because we print for every business on the same street and we will not
  take sides
- work that needs a lift or scaffolding above 6 metres, because we do not own the kit and we will
  not carry someone else's risk
- anyone who wants us to print artwork they do not own the rights to, because we are the ones who
  get the letter

## Where to send people we turn down

- One-off personal jobs: the print counter at the stationery shop in town does single boards and
  small frames.
- Rush work: say no plainly and give the first date we can actually hit.
- Political campaign work and rights problems: a kind no, nowhere to send them.
